The Problem

Why Your Sink
Dropped Away

Undermount sinks on granite or stone are mounted from below using adhesive and small metal clips. Under the weight of a full sink, heat from hot pots, and years of moisture cycling, that system eventually lets go. It's not your fault — it's a flawed installation standard.

Weight Cycles

Repeated loading from dishes and water stresses adhesive bonds never designed to hold indefinitely.

Heat Damage

Hot pots and steam slowly degrade the epoxy between the sink rim and the underside of the stone.

Moisture Intrusion

Once the seal starts to go, water wicks into the cabinet top — swelling the wood and accelerating the drop.

Inadequate Install

Most sinks were put in with minimal clips and basic epoxy — not built to last 10–15 years under real use.

The Fix

We Don't Just Put It Back.
We Fix What Failed.

1

Disconnect & Assess

Plumbing and disposal disconnected. We check the sink rail and cabinet top for water damage before finalizing scope — no surprises after we start.

2

Clean & Repair

Old adhesive scraped off the stone underside. Swollen or rotted wood dried and repaired or replaced as needed.

3

Install Proper Support

We fabricate and install the GPPS Structural Sink Support System™ — a custom plywood ledger with steel brackets anchored to the cabinet frame. Weight transfers into the structure, not the adhesive. This is what most repairs skip.

4

Re-Set & Seal

Fresh two-part epoxy, new clips, sink pulled tight to the countertop. Perimeter sealed to keep moisture out of the seam.

5

Reconnect & Confirm

Plumbing and disposal back in, everything tested. You're in service with a sink that's actually supported.

Worth Knowing Upfront

Our installation includes ½" plywood reinforcement on the cabinet sides to ensure we're anchoring into material with known strength — regardless of what the original substrate is. The support brackets and plywood together take up a modest amount of space inside the cabinet, typically 2–3 inches of vertical clearance directly under the sink opening. For most cabinets this doesn't affect daily use, but we'll walk you through exactly what goes in and where before we start. No surprises.
